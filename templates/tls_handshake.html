    Analogy: mixing paint
    Both start with the same public base colour. Each mixes in a secret colour and swaps the mixture. Adding their own secret again gives both the same final colour - but an observer who saw only the mixtures cannot un-mix them.

    Certificates authenticate - they don't encrypt your data

    The certificate proves identity. Session keys encrypt the data.

    Technical details: CertificateVerify
    The certificate binds shop.example.com to a public key. In CertificateVerify the server signs the handshake with the matching private key, proving it is genuine. Your page data is encrypted with the symmetric session keys from the key exchange.
    Why "ephemeral" matters (forward secrecy)
    The DH keys are thrown away after the session. Even if the server's long-term private key is stolen later, recorded traffic cannot be decrypted.
    What this demo simplifies
    Real TLS 1.3 derives keys through a longer HKDF key schedule that mixes in a hash of every handshake message; here one HKDF step per key is shown. Key shares really are sent in ClientHello and ServerHello - the "Key Exchange" stage shows the local computation.
