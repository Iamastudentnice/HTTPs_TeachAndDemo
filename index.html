    Learn more: what HTTPS does and does not protect

    It protects your data in transit. The contents of what you send and receive are encrypted, and any change made on the way is detected.

    It does not hide everything. The network can still see which website you connect to, your IP address, and when and how much data is sent.

    The certificate proves identity, it does not do the encrypting. It lets your browser check it is talking to the real website. The data itself is encrypted with a separate secret key agreed at the start (see Lab 03).

    The padlock is about the connection, not the website. Scam sites can use HTTPS too, and HTTPS cannot protect a device that already has malware.

    Watching is not the same as tampering. The eavesdropper here only watches (a passive attack). Attackers who actively change or fake traffic are covered in the MITM lab.
